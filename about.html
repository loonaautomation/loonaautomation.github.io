About Loona Automation

Free tools and AI services, built by a teacher in Pakistan.

Loona Automation builds free online tools and practical AI services for ordinary people — students, teachers, freelancers, shopkeepers, office workers and families — who need a job done quickly, without paying for software, creating accounts, or handing over their personal files. Every tool on this site works in your browser, costs nothing, and was designed around one simple test: does it solve a real problem, in the fewest possible steps?

The Story

Loona Automation was founded by Aurang Zeb Loona, a school teacher and AI teacher trainer from Jaranwala, in the Faisalabad district of Punjab, Pakistan. His working life has two halves. In the classroom, he teaches students and trains fellow teachers to use artificial intelligence in their own classrooms — showing them, patiently and in plain language, how AI can prepare lessons, notes and questions instead of replacing the teacher. Outside the classroom, he builds the tools he wished existed: first for his own students and colleagues, then for anyone on the internet who has the same problem.

That double life shapes everything Loona makes. A teacher learns quickly that if a tool takes more than a minute to understand, people quietly stop using it. So Loona tools are deliberately simple on the surface and careful underneath: no sign-ups, no downloads, no confusing dashboards — open the page, do the job, leave with your result.

The Mission: Bolti Duniya

Behind the tools sits a bigger mission called Bolti Duniya — "the speaking world." Its belief can be stated in one sentence:

"Every book should speak — in every language — for everyone who cannot read."

Millions of people — because of illiteracy, visual impairment, dyslexia, age, or simply because their language is underserved — are locked out of the knowledge sitting inside ordinary printed books. Loona's long-term work is aimed at that wall: tools that turn photographed pages into spoken words, notes that organise themselves by class, subject and chapter, and AI assistance that meets people in the language they actually speak. The free tools on this site are the foundation of that mission — they build the audience, the trust and the means to keep going.

What Loona Builds

Free online tools. Loona's tool websites include QR code generators for thirty different purposes — WiFi, WhatsApp, visiting cards, menus, payments and more — a collection of seventy calculators covering everyday needs from zakat, age and BMI to EMI loans, CGPA and percentage, an image compressor that shrinks photos to the exact kilobyte limits that job, visa and university forms demand, an invoice generator for freelancers and small businesses, and a growing set of study and productivity apps. Most of these tools process your files on your own device; your photos and documents are not uploaded to our servers. Step-by-step guides on this site explain the most common tasks in plain words.

AI services. For schools, clinics, shops and small businesses, Loona designs practical automation: AI assistants that answer customer questions, voice and calling agents that handle appointment calls, systems that turn paperwork and notes into organised documents, and training sessions that help teachers and teams use AI confidently in their daily work. The emphasis is always on usefulness over novelty — automation that quietly removes repetitive work.

The Websites, One by One

The network has grown into 30 websites, each with a single clear job. The calculators site alone holds 292 calculators and converters; the salary and tax site holds 80 tools built on official labour-law and tax tables for the Gulf, Pakistan and Europe; the image site holds 53 tools centred on compressing photographs to exact kilobyte limits; and the remaining collections cover PDF work (39 tools), text work (47), developer utilities (71), construction estimates and electrical calculators (38), audio work (23), business documents (22 generators), study tools for students and teachers (49), a design studio (80 tools), Islamic references, US timesheets, QR codes and a resume builder. New tools usually begin as a reader's email: someone describes a recurring annoyance — a portal that rejects their document, a payslip they cannot verify, an archive file their computer cannot open — and if a small, free, private tool can remove that annoyance for everyone, it gets built, tested against hand-worked examples, and added to the collection it belongs to. That is also why the guides on this site exist: whenever the same question arrives three times, it earns a written answer, with the arithmetic shown, that anyone can check. The network is maintained continuously behind the scenes — search indexing, corrections and rule updates continue whether or not anyone is watching — because a tool that quietly goes out of date is worse than no tool at all.

Why Is Everything Free?

Because the people who need these tools most are often the people least able to pay for them: a student compressing a photo for an admission form at midnight, a small shopkeeper making his first invoice, a teacher preparing tomorrow's lesson. Charging them per use would defeat the purpose.

The site is funded by advertising. The tools stay free for everyone, and advertisers — through programmes such as Google AdSense — cover the costs of running and improving them. That is the whole bargain, stated plainly: you use the tools free; unobtrusive ads keep the lights on. If a tool saved you time, telling a friend about it helps us more than anything else.

Who Uses Loona Tools?

The honest answer is: people in the middle of something. A graduate in Lahore at 11 p.m., shrinking her photograph because the admission portal closes at midnight. A shopkeeper in Karachi making a proper invoice for the first time because a big customer asked for one. A café owner in Islamabad who is tired of spelling the WiFi password forty times a day. A teacher in a small town preparing a lesson with AI help, in the language her students think in. Loona is built for those moments — practical, slightly urgent, and very human — and our guides exist so that nobody has to figure the process out alone.

If you are a student, teacher, freelancer or small business owner with a recurring annoyance that a simple tool could remove, you are exactly who we build for — and we read every suggestion that arrives.

How We Work

· Free, no sign-up. If a task needs an account, it is the wrong design.
· Private by design. Wherever possible, work happens in your browser, on your device.
· Built for real conditions. Pages are light and fast because many of our users are on modest phones and slow connections.
· Honest limits. We say what a tool cannot do — for example, our educational guides are information, not professional or religious rulings — and we point you to qualified people when a matter needs them.
