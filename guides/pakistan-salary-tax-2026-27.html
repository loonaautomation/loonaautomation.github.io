Pakistan Salary Tax 2026-27, Explained with Worked Examples

By Aurang Zeb Loona — Loona Automation

Every July, Pakistan's salaried workers meet a new tax table, and every July the same confusion follows: payslips change, colleagues compare deductions, and many people quietly assume the worst. For Tax Year 2026-27 — in force since 1 July 2026 under the Finance Act 2026 — the salaried table changed in ways that actually favour middle incomes. This guide sets out the current slabs, works two complete salaries through them, and clears up the misunderstandings that make the tax look bigger than it is.

This is general information about how the salaried slabs work, not personal tax advice. Individual cases can involve other income, credits and adjustments that only your payroll department or a tax adviser can settle.

The TY 2026-27 Salaried Slabs

The table below applies where salary is more than 75% of a person's taxable income — the normal situation for employees. Tax is calculated on annual salary, and your employer withholds one-twelfth of it each month under section 149 of the Income Tax Ordinance.

  • Up to Rs 600,000 a year: 0% — no tax.
  • Rs 600,001 – 1,200,000: 1% of the amount above 600,000. (Tax at Rs 1.2 million = Rs 6,000.)
  • Rs 1,200,001 – 2,200,000: Rs 6,000 + 11% of the amount above 1,200,000.
  • Rs 2,200,001 – 3,200,000: Rs 116,000 + 20% of the amount above 2,200,000.
  • Rs 3,200,001 – 4,100,000: Rs 316,000 + 25% of the amount above 3,200,000.
  • Rs 4,100,001 – 5,600,000: Rs 541,000 + 29% of the amount above 4,100,000.
  • Rs 5,600,001 – 7,000,000: Rs 976,000 + 32% of the amount above 5,600,000.
  • Above Rs 7,000,000: Rs 1,424,000 + 35% of the amount above 7,000,000.

What changed from 2025-26: the middle bands softened (20% and 25% where last year charged 23% and 30%), the 35% top rate now starts at Rs 7 million instead of Rs 4.1 million, two new bands (29% and 32%) were inserted — and the surcharge on high salaries was abolished for salaried persons. Last year a 9% surcharge applied to the tax of salaried people earning above Rs 10 million; for 2026-27 it is simply gone.

Worked Example 1: Rs 175,000 a Month

Annual salary = 175,000 × 12 = Rs 2,100,000. Walk it through the bands: the first 600,000 is free; the next 600,000 costs 1% = Rs 6,000; the remaining 900,000 sits in the 11% band and costs 99,000. Total annual tax = 6,000 + 99,000 = Rs 105,000, so the monthly deduction is 105,000 ÷ 12 = Rs 8,750, the effective rate is exactly 5% of salary, and take-home pay is Rs 166,250 a month.

Worked Example 2: Rs 400,000 a Month

Annual salary = 400,000 × 12 = Rs 4,800,000. This salary passes through the free band and the 1%, 11%, 20% and 25% bands — collecting Rs 541,000 of tax by the time it reaches Rs 4,100,000 — and its top 700,000 is charged at 29% = Rs 203,000. Total annual tax = 541,000 + 203,000 = Rs 744,000; monthly deduction Rs 62,000; effective rate 15.5%. Note the lesson the two examples teach together: even at Rs 400,000 a month, with a headline "top rate" of 29%, the share of the whole salary actually paid in tax is 15.5% — because most of the salary was taxed at the lower rates on the way up.

Common Mistakes and Misunderstandings

  • "My whole salary is taxed at my top rate." The most widespread error. Slabs are marginal: each rate touches only the slice of income inside its band, as both examples show. Nobody's entire salary is taxed at 29% or 35%.
  • Using last year's table. A large share of the calculators and forwarded spreadsheets still circulating carry the 2025-26 rates — including a surcharge that no longer exists for salaried staff. Check that any tool you use says "Finance Act 2026" or "TY 2026-27".
  • Mixing up the salaried and non-salaried tables. Business and professional income uses a different, generally heavier table. If most of your income is salary, the salaried table is yours; if most is not, it is not.
  • Assuming "no tax" means "nothing to file". A salary under Rs 600,000 owes no tax, but filing a return is a separate obligation with its own rules and benefits (such as appearing on the Active Taxpayers List). Do not confuse the two questions.
  • Forgetting that bonuses count. A bonus is salary: it is added to the annual figure and taxed at your marginal band, which is why a bonus month shows a heavier deduction.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax is deducted on Rs 100,000 a month?

Rs 100,000 × 12 = Rs 1,200,000 a year, which produces Rs 6,000 of tax for the whole year — about Rs 500 a month in TY 2026-27.

I changed jobs mid-year. How is my tax handled?

Each employer withholds based on the salary they pay you, projecting it across the year. When you file your annual return, the two employments are combined and any difference between what was withheld and what the full-year table demands is settled then — which is why keeping both employers' salary certificates matters.

Can anything legitimately reduce the tax my payroll deducts?

The law provides specific credits and adjustments — for example in connection with certain approved investments and donations — which payroll can apply when you declare and document them. They are worth asking your HR or finance department about, because undeclared items generally cannot be applied mid-year.
